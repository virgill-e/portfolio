# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Whoever lands on the site — recruiters, other developers, curious visitors from social or search. No single primary audience and no client-facing or freelance intent: this is a personal showcase, not a service pitch. Visitors are browsing to see what the person behind the name can build and how they think about craft.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that shows the range and quality of Virgile's work: shipped projects, technical range, and attention to visual/motion detail. Success means a visitor leaves believing the work is genuinely crafted, remembers a project, and optionally follows up via a social link or email.

## Positioning

Creative attention to detail — a developer who treats interface and motion as part of the craft, not an afterthought bolted onto the code.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: Explore the work (scroll into the projects section) is primary; social/contact links (email, GitHub, LinkedIn, X, Instagram) in the footer are the secondary, low-friction fallback.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: this person sweats the visual and motion details most developers skip.
- Belief ladder: (1) this feels hand-crafted, not templated; (2) the projects on show prove real technical range; (3) following up (a link, an email) is worth doing.
- Proof on hand: the shipped project case studies already in the projects section (Noshaq, PIkemon, qairn mobile, vidiledger, Clove, too many cookies, tv-time).

## Brand Personality

Bold, experimental, and striking — willing to take visual risks rather than settle for safe. Voice is confident and understated in copy, loud in craft (motion, layout, type).

## Anti-references

Generic corporate/agency polish: the overly-slick, safe, interchangeable SaaS-template look. Nothing that reads as a Webflow-style default.

## Design Principles

- Motion as narrative: every animation should reveal or emphasize something, not decorate.
- Craft over convention: default choices (cards, generic grids) are the fallback, not the goal — earn them or replace them.
- Show, don't tell: the projects and the interactions prove the "creative attention to detail" claim; copy stays out of the way.
- One bold gesture per section, not uniform loudness everywhere.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific requirements stated; hold to WCAG AA baseline (contrast, focus states) and respect `prefers-reduced-motion` given the animation-heavy design.
